Monthly updating experiment, 2026-11-06

Scope: US domestic operating carrier (FORM-1) monthly records from 2019 onward.
Original source is unchanged. Target = mishandled bags / enplaned bags * 1000.
This is NOT permanent-loss probability, passenger claim frequency or Cathay risk.
2019 initializes history; supervised examples begin in 2020.

Features: carrier ID, calendar month sine/cosine, month trend, latest available
rate, latest three-month mean and population standard deviation, latest rate
minus preceding three-month mean, previous-year same-month rate, latest bag
volume, latest month-on-month volume change. Lags are calendar joins, not row
shifts; absent history is excluded, never replaced with zero.

RF: 300 trees, min_samples_leaf=8, random_state=42, direct rate regression.
No log-target transformation in this experiment. Equal training-row weights.
Carrier one-hot encoding; test unknown carrier categories ignored by encoder.
No hyperparameter search or changes based on 2026 results.

Two data-availability scenarios:
d=1: target t may use data through t-1, an ideal prompt-availability scenario.
d=3: target t may use data through t-3, an assumed publication-delay sensitivity.
Neither reconstructs actual BTS publication dates or historical data revisions.

Training-window selection uses rolling predictions in 2025, comparing an
expanding window with the most recent 36 target months. Select by baggage-volume
weighted absolute rate error. d=1 uses Jan-Dec 2025 validation (168 rows).
d=3 uses Jan-Oct 2025 validation (140 rows), excluding outcomes that would not
yet be available at the first 2026 forecast origin. Both select expanding.

Test: Jan-Aug 2026, identical 101 carrier-month rows in each comparison.
No 2026 records excluded for missing features. Carrier coverage varies:
Spirit has five months; the other twelve carriers have eight months each.
Fixed RF: fit once at the first 2026 origin; never refit during testing.
Updating RF: refit each target month on labels available through t-d.
BOTH receive refreshed, identical predictors for each forecast month. This
isolates the effect of retraining. Fixed RF is NOT an all-information-frozen
year-ahead forecast; its later inputs may include already observed 2026 data.
Updating RF likewise uses earlier 2026 outcomes only after the assumed delay.
The previous experiment had different predictors and a log-transformed target;
do not attribute differences from that experiment solely to one new feature.

Main metric: volume-weighted MAE, in mishandled bags per 1000 enplaned bags.
Test volumes are used only for evaluation weights, never as predictor inputs.
                     d=1          d=3
Last-year same month 0.860345     0.860345
Recent 3-month mean  0.940228     1.190569
Fixed RF            0.646944     0.723527
Monthly refit RF    0.654087     0.765321

Monthly refitting beats the fixed RF in only one of the seven months after
January in each availability scenario. January is tied (same training data).
Updating RF is about 24.0% better than seasonal naive under d=1, but about
1.1% worse than fixed RF. Under d=3 it is about 5.8% worse than fixed RF.
These are observed point estimates, not statistically significant claims.
Both unweighted MAE and RMSE also favor fixed RF over monthly refitting here.

Conclusions: periodic learning has been implemented and evaluated. This test
does not show a benefit from automatic monthly replacement of fitted parameters.
It supports evaluating new candidates before replacing a deployed model, but
that gated replacement strategy has NOT itself been tested here.
Eight months, correlated carrier-months, pandemic history, changing carrier
coverage and a single RF seed limit inference. No confidence intervals given.
2026 results have now been inspected, so further tuning on them would make this
an exploratory evaluation, not an untouched final test.
No claim severity, insurance price, Cathay transfer or operational reduction
in mishandling has been validated by this experiment.
