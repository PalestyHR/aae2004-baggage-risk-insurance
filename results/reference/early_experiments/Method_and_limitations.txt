BTS baggage forecasting: initial temporal holdout experiment
Run date: 2026-11-06

SOURCE AND SCOPE
User-supplied T_MISHANDLED_BAGGAGE.csv, 2161 rows, 2016-2026.
The latest observation is August 2026. Preserve the source unchanged.
Retain 2019 onward, FORM-1 operating-carrier records only (1442 rows).
FORM-2 codeshare records are excluded to avoid mixing reporting populations.
Before 2019, the reported measure was passenger reports, not the current bag count.
Mishandled baggage includes lost, delayed, damaged and pilfered baggage.
This is US domestic carrier-month data, not Cathay data or passenger claims data.

TARGETS
1. Monthly mishandled bag count per operating carrier.
2. Monthly mishandled bags per 1000 enplaned bags.
Rates use baggage exposure, not passengers. Missing values are not zeros.

DESIGN
Each feature row uses carrier ID, month sine/cosine, year trend, and the same
carrier's previous-year same-month count, rate and enplaned baggage volume.
All 2026 features are based on calendar information and 2025 observations.
No actual 2026 counts, volumes or rates are used for prediction or training.
No within-2026 updating occurs. This is a frozen-model multi-horizon test,
not a rolling one-month-ahead deployment simulation.
216 rows without prior-year same-month history are excluded, including the
2019 initialization year. No 2026 rows are excluded by this rule.
Training examples 2020-2024: 957; validation 2025: 168.
Select count model by validation WAPE and rate model by exposure-weighted MAE.
Refit on 2020-2025 (1125 examples), test on 2026 Jan-Aug (101 examples).
2019 observations supply lagged features. 2026 observations are never used
to choose models or hyperparameters. All candidate test scores are retained.

MODELS
Seasonal naive: use last year's same-month value.
Ridge: alpha=10, standardized numerical inputs and one-hot carrier ID.
Random forest: 300 trees, minimum leaf size 8, random_state=42.
Both learned models fit log1p(target), then invert with expm1 and clip at zero.
The resulting predictions are not guaranteed conditional mean estimates;
log transformation can bias aggregate forecasts. Rate is capped at 1000.
This is a small initial comparison, not an exhaustive model search.

RESULTS
Both targets selected random forest using 2025 validation only.
2026 count WAPE: naive 21.5609%, ridge 48.6343%, random forest 21.1300%.
Count MAE: naive 3118.66, ridge 7034.68, random forest 3056.33 bags/carrier-month.
Count RMSE: naive 5144.35, ridge 10874.75, random forest 5103.11.
Rate exposure-weighted MAE per 1000 bags:
naive 0.860345, ridge 0.984765, random forest 0.761723.
WAPE = sum(abs(predicted-observed))/sum(observed); it is NOT classification
accuracy and 1-WAPE must not be described as predictive accuracy.
Exposure weights use test volumes for evaluation ONLY, not as predictors.

LIMITATIONS
101 carrier-month observations are correlated, not 101 independent trials.
Carrier coverage varies by month; totals describe observed records only.
Only eight 2026 months are available. No confidence intervals or evidence of
statistical significance has been established. Count improvement is small.
Pandemic years are included and may cause structural instability.
This retrospectively uses a downloaded historical snapshot. Publication lags
and historical revisions are not reconstructed; it is not a real-time-vintage
backtest available on 1 January 2026.
Carrier-level monthly counts do not reveal route, passenger, delay duration,
permanent loss, insurance purchase, claim eligibility or claim severity.
US domestic results cannot establish performance for Cathay's network.
Rate per bag-segment is not the probability a passenger claims on an itinerary.
Pricing needs separately justified coverage, frequency conversion, severity,
selection effects and risk assumptions. Do not equate mishandling with payout.
